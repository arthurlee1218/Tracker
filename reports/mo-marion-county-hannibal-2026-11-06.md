# Hannibal, Marion County, MO: Data Center Siting Brief

As of 2026-11-06 · Built from Data Center Watch entries and sentiment data, checked against public reporting · Not legal advice; confirm with local counsel

**Bottom line.** Hannibal has no pause, no moratorium and no named project. It has actively marketed itself: the city utility's Board of Public Works issued a request for proposals (RFP) for up to 100 MW of power at the city-owned Lakeside Technology Park, and data centers are a named target use. In July 2026 the city said it had received several unsolicited inquiries, had chosen no company, and favored an RFP process. The council had not discussed the matter. The biggest lever is local: the city owns the power utility, so council and the utility board decide who gets service, and the state utility regulator's large-load rules do not reach it. The hard limits are the river-fed water plant and what the city's own power contracts can supply. The next visible step is staff presenting feasibility findings at a public meeting. I could not confirm a date for it.

## 1. Legal analysis

**Local status.** Data Center Watch logs two local entries, both pending. On 2026-03-20 the Board of Public Works issued the RFP for up to 100 MW of firm retail power at Lakeside Technology Park, with questions due May 8 and proposals due May 15 ([Hannibal Regional Economic Development Council](https://hredc.com/news/now-open-100-mw-power-opportunity-in-the-hannibal-region/)). I could not confirm whether any proposals were received. On 2026-07-30 the city said no company was selected and nothing had gone to council ([Hannibal Courier-Post](https://hannibal.net/2026/08/01/hannibal-officials-say-no-decisions-made-on-rumored-data-center-project/)). It said it would present feasibility findings publicly and hold meetings and hearings before any final decision. The article does not say whether the new RFP is the March one or a separate one.

**State preemption and zoning.** I could not confirm Missouri's rules on local data center bans or moratoriums, or Hannibal's zoning code. The tracker logs no state action on data centers for Missouri. Ask counsel whether the city can adopt a pause, and by what process.

**Power regulation.** Missouri's SB 4 (2025) directed the Public Service Commission to make large-load customers cover their share of costs. The commission approved large-load tariffs for Ameren and Evergy at a 75 MW threshold, with 12-year minimum terms, an 80% minimum demand charge and two years of collateral ([Missouri PSC](https://psc.mo.gov/General/Utility_Tariffs_for_Large_Load_Customers)). Reporting notes that the commission oversees only investor-owned utilities, not city-run utilities or cooperatives. Hannibal's utility is a city division, so those tariff protections would not automatically apply. Any comparable terms would be a local decision.

**Incentives.** State law offers a sales and use tax exemption for data centers. A new facility needs at least $25 million of investment and ten new jobs within 36 months, at an average wage of at least 150% of the county average ([RSMo 144.810](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=144.810)). The city estimated that a small data center could produce tens of millions of dollars a year in tax revenue, with much of it going to the school district. That is the city's estimate, and I could not check it. I did not research local abatement terms.

**Citizen initiatives.** I could not confirm initiative or referendum rules for Hannibal's charter. Hannibal voters have used the ballot before: a 2016 proposition barred ammonia in the water treatment system ([Hannibal BPW](https://www.hannibalbpw.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/BPW_Annual_Report_2015-2016_FINAL.pdf)).

**Outlook.** The state summary notes about a dozen Missouri counties and cities have paused, including Pettis County for up to two years. A Hannibal pause is possible but nothing is logged.

## 3. Public sentiment

**What the data shows.** The tracker has 41 saved posts for Hannibal, all on Facebook, dated 2026-05-01 to 2026-11-04. Of these, 32 are against, 7 are supportive and 2 are mixed. These are people who post online, not residents, and engagement measures reach, not agreement. Most posts are comments under two group threads, so they are not a sample of the town.

**The main concerns, explained.**
- **Water (10 posts: 8 against, 2 supportive).** One commenter says they do not want a center and have read articles about it. A supportive commenter says recirculating systems solved the water issue. Whether that holds depends on the cooling design, which no one has published. The river intake and 7.5 MGD plant rating are checkable. Demand is unknown.
- **Rates (8 posts: 7 against, 1 mixed).** Commenters fear higher electric bills and one says the utility just raised its price. I could not verify that. It is checkable with the utility's rate schedule. Because the utility is city-owned, rates are set locally.
- **Power (6 posts: 4 against, 1 supportive, 1 mixed).** The concern is the grid's ability to serve a large load. The RFP states 100 MW is available, which the utility, not residents, would need to substantiate.
- **Land (5 posts: 4 against, 1 mixed).** Concerns are about use of the land and the area around it. The RFP describes more than 100 acres of industrial land. Noise (3 posts, all against) and jobs (3 posts: 1 against, 1 supportive, 1 mixed) are smaller threads. Claims that data centers create many jobs are contested. The state tax exemption requires only ten new jobs.

## 4. Other good-to-knows

- Missouri's large-load tariffs for Ameren and Evergy are useful templates for ratepayer protections (75 MW threshold, 12-year terms, 80% minimum bill). Consumer advocates say they fall short and want longer contracts and higher minimums.
- Pettis County, Missouri, paused for up to two years. It is the closest logged precedent for a local pause in the state.
- Watch for the city's feasibility presentation, any RFP award or council agenda item, and any rate action by the Board of Public Works. I could not confirm dates for these.
- Open questions to put to the city: which companies inquired, whether proposals were received, and the water and power draw of any proposal.
