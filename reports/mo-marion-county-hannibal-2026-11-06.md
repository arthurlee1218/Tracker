# Hannibal, Marion County, MO: Data Center Siting Brief

As of 2026-11-06 · Built from Data Center Watch entries and sentiment data, checked against public reporting · Not legal advice; confirm with local counsel

**Bottom line.** Hannibal has no pause and no named data center project. The city is courting large power users, though. Its Board of Public Works offered up to 100 MW at the city-owned Lakeside Technology Park, with proposals due May 15, 2026. In July the city said it had received several unsolicited inquiries, had chosen no company, and preferred a request-for-proposals route. The council had not discussed the matter. The biggest legal lever is the city's own zoning and utility control. Cities are not bound by county zoning, so what happens inside Hannibal is largely the council's decision. The hard limits are power, water and public acceptance, and I found no Hannibal-specific water figures. The next signal is whether the city presents its feasibility findings at a public meeting. Online sentiment is mostly opposed.

## 1. Legal analysis

**Local status.** The tracker logs two local entries and no pause or ordinance. The utility board's RFP (issued March 20, 2026) offers up to 100 MW of firm capacity from the city's municipally owned utilities, with an on-site 161 kV transmission and substation, and names data centers, advanced manufacturing and high-performance computing as targets ([HREDC](https://hredc.com/news/now-open-100-mw-power-opportunity-in-the-hannibal-region/)). Questions were due May 8 and proposals May 15. I could not confirm whether any proposals were received, and I could not review the full RFP document for incentive terms. On [Aug. 1, 2026 the Courier-Post reported](https://hannibal.net/2026/08/01/hannibal-officials-say-no-decisions-made-on-rumored-data-center-project/) that the city had chosen no company and that nothing had gone to council. Staff would present feasibility findings at a public meeting, and any advancing project would get public meetings and hearings. The article does not say whether the new RFP is the same as the March one.

**County versus city zoning.** [WGEM reported](https://www.wgem.com/2026/07/15/palmyra-hannibal-officials-address-data-center-rumors/) that a Marion County moratorium blocked data center construction until guidelines were developed, and that Hannibal and Palmyra, as cities, are not bound by county zoning. Presiding Commissioner David Lomax said the county lacks infrastructure for large-scale data centers. A search summary also indicated the county commission revised its land-use order on July 27, lifted the moratorium, required M-1 industrial zoning for energy projects of 5 MW or more, and formed a task force. I could not open that article, so treat it as unconfirmed. Either way, it applies to unincorporated areas, not Hannibal.

**Eminent domain.** City Attorney James Lemon said Hannibal would not cooperate with any entity seeking to take residents' land through eminent domain, and called the local industrial park better suited to data centers than other locations ([WGEM](https://www.wgem.com/2026/07/15/palmyra-hannibal-officials-address-data-center-rumors/)).

**State rules.** Missouri's SB 4 (2025) directed the Public Service Commission to set large-load rates so other customers do not absorb the costs. Tariffs are approved for Ameren Missouri and Evergy, and a Liberty Utilities case (ET-2026-0184) is pending. Core terms are a minimum 12-year service period, an optional 5-year ramp (up to 17 years), an 80% demand-charge minimum and collateral equal to two years of minimum bills ([Missouri PSC](https://psc.mo.gov/General/Utility_Tariffs_for_Large_Load_Customers)). The PSC page does not say whether municipal utilities are covered, and I could not confirm that. Hannibal's power is municipal, so these tariffs likely do not directly govern it. The tracker logs no state action on data centers. I could not confirm Missouri's rules on local moratoriums, initiative and referendum for city zoning, or water law, and did not research them in depth.

**Outlook.** The council has said it will hold hearings before deciding. Any RFP result, or a council agenda item, would be the trigger for formal action.

## 2. Practical hurdles

| Hurdle | Why it matters | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Power | The city sells up to 100 MW from its own utilities. Wholesale supply and transmission upgrades beyond the on-site 161 kV substation are not documented in what I opened. | Ask the Board of Public Works for supply arrangements, rates and contract terms. |
| Water | Water is the top topic in online posts (10 of 41). I found no Hannibal water capacity or usage figures. | Request the city's water capacity data and cooling-water plans early. |
| Local politics | The city says it will hold hearings, and rumors already drew pushback. | Engage officials and residents before any filing; follow the city's stated RFP process. |
| Site control | Lakeside Technology Park is city-owned, so the city is both seller and regulator. | Expect transparency demands; deal through the RFP, not private talks. |
| County and state shift | County rules do not apply inside the city, but neighboring unincorporated land is subject to county land-use rules. | Check the jurisdiction of any parcel. |

## 3. Public sentiment

The tracker's online data (May 1 to Nov. 4, 2026; Bluesky, Reddit, X and meeting-minute comments) has 41 posts: 32 against, 7 for and 2 mixed. Top topics are water (10), rates (8), power (6), land (5), noise (3) and jobs (3). These are people who post online, not residents, and cannot be read as a poll.

Offline, I found no election, poll or organized campaign. The city has framed a small data center as potentially producing tens of millions of dollars a year in tax revenue, much of it for the school district and other taxing bodies ([Courier-Post](https://hannibal.net/2026/08/01/hannibal-officials-say-no-decisions-made-on-rumored-data-center-project/)). The opposing arguments in the data are water, rates and land use. Palmyra's officials said no data centers are coming there and formed a task force.

## 4. Other good-to-knows

- The main reference is Hannibal's own RFP: 100+ acres, 161 kV on site, up to 100 MW ([HREDC](https://hredc.com/news/now-open-100-mw-power-opportunity-in-the-hannibal-region/)).
- Gardner, MO: developer Beale Infrastructure withdrew its data center application in May after hundreds of residents opposed it. Independence passed setbacks and a generator-testing cap. These are Kansas City-area precedents, reported in a search summary I could not open.
- Neighbors: Palmyra (task force) and Marion County (land-use changes and task force, per the unconfirmed report above).
- Watch for a council feasibility presentation, any RFP selection, and Liberty and Ameren rate proceedings.
- Cost-allocation fights continue: consumer advocates want longer contracts and a 95% demand charge, while PSC staff say the safeguards are enough.
