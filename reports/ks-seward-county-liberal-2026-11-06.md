# Liberal, Seward County, KS: Data Center Siting Brief

As of 2026-11-06 · Built from Data Center Watch entries and sentiment data, checked against public reporting · Not legal advice; confirm with local counsel

**Bottom line.** Data Center Watch has no logged entries, no named projects and no saved social posts for Liberal or Seward County, and I found no reporting of a proposed data center here. No pause, moratorium or local rules are on record. The biggest levers are state-level: Kansas offers a 20-year sales tax exemption for large data centers and Evergy has a large-load tariff, but the local utility here appears to be a different company, so that tariff may not apply. The hard limits are water (the Ogallala aquifer, with proposed mandatory pumping cuts) and power supply. The most likely near-term change is the fallout from the Nov. 3 votes in Emporia, El Dorado and Ottawa, which could encourage similar petitions elsewhere in the state.

## 1. Legal analysis

**Local status.** The tracker holds 0 entries for this place. I could not confirm any Liberal or Seward County moratorium, zoning rule or data center ordinance. Check with the city and county planning offices before relying on this.

**State picture.** The tracker's state summary (reviewed 2026-11-04) says regulators approved Evergy's large-load tariff in 2025, and that Emporia, Ottawa and El Dorado voters passed strict local measures on Nov. 3. Per [KAKE-area reporting summarized in search results](https://www.kake.com/news/el-dorado-emporia-voters-pass-bans-on-high-impact-data-centers/article_aaa685e6-bf3d-4995-b055-36b3e3a11af8.html) (I did not open this page), El Dorado approved a 10-year ban and Ottawa a 25 MW cap. I could not confirm whether Kansas law preempts local bans; that is the key legal question to ask counsel.

**Citizen initiatives.** [KVOE](https://kvoe.com/2026/11/03/10-year-ban-on-high-impact-data-centers-and-type-2-and-3-battery-energy-storage-systems-pass-on-election-night-quarter-percent-sales-tax-question-for-new-and-updated-emporia-rec-facilities-fail/) reports unofficial Emporia results of 4,346 yes to 2,891 no. A Lyon County judge has not yet ruled on whether the petition was legislative or administrative. If administrative, it will most likely be void. That ruling will shape whether similar petitions work in other Kansas cities. I did not research Liberal's own charter or petition rules.

**Incentives.** Under [SB 98](https://www.kansascommerce.gov/program/business-incentives-and-services/sb-98-data-center-sales-tax-exemption/), a hyperscale operator must invest at least $250 million in eligible costs within five years of opening, create 20 new full-time jobs filled by Kansas residents within two years, sign a 10-year power purchase agreement with the utility certified for the site, and adopt a water plan. The page describes no local role beyond a state fusion center review.

**Power regulation.** The [KCC release of Nov. 6, 2025](https://www.kcc.ks.gov/news-11-6-25) approved Evergy's Large Load Power Service for loads above 75 MW: a minimum 12-year term (plus up to 5 years of ramp), a minimum bill of 80% of contract demand, and collateral of two years of minimum bills. The release names only Evergy.

**Outlook.** Expect the 2027 session and the Emporia court ruling to matter most. I could not confirm specific bills.
