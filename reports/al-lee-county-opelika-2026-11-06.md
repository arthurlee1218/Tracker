# Opelika, Lee County, AL: Data Center Siting Brief

As of 2026-11-06 · Built from Data Center Watch entries and sentiment data, checked against public reporting · Not legal advice; confirm with local counsel

**Bottom line.** I found no data center project proposed in Opelika, and no adopted data center pause or ordinance. Residents have pressed the council at several meetings (April 21 to June 16) for limits, and the council took no action in that period. A late-October wave of rumors about "multiple data centers" spread in local Facebook groups; I could not confirm any named project behind it. The biggest lever is local zoning and incentive votes, since Alabama's new state law (effective Oct. 1) only reaches contracts of 150 MW or more, and only through the Public Service Commission (PSC). Most likely to change soon: a zoning, conditional-use or moratorium proposal if a site is named, and any incentive vote for an industrial-park project.

## 1. Legal analysis

**Local status.** The tracker logs one Opelika entry: residents spoke against data centers at council meetings from April 21 to June 16, citing water, power and rumored sites, and asking for a moratorium or protections ([May 19 minutes](https://opelikaal.portal.civicclerk.com/event/663/files); [June 16 minutes](https://opelikaal.portal.civicclerk.com/event/667/files)). The city's Planning & Zoning page lists a "2026 - 2027 Moratorium" reports section with a baseline report, survey results as of July 1, 2026 and a July workshop summary ([city page](https://www.opelika-al.gov/1251/Moratorium-Reports---Sharing-Progress-Wi)). That page does not say what the moratorium covers or mention data centers, so I could not confirm whether it applies to them.

**State law.** A state law effective Oct. 1 (Act 2026-610 as reported) requires the PSC to review whether contracts with data centers of 150 MW and up recover their full cost. The PSC adopted its review procedure on Oct. 6, with a vote due within 90 days of a filing ([Alabama Reflector](https://alabamareflector.com/2026/10/07/public-service-commission-green-lights-new-process-for-agreements-for-data-centers/) was not openable for me; details come from search summaries and the tracker's state record). I could not confirm whether the law covers municipal utilities such as Opelika Power Services, which is city-run by its name and page branding but whose page does not state ownership or supplier.

**Preemption and initiatives.** I could not confirm whether Alabama restricts local data center bans or moratoriums, or what citizen initiative rights exist for Opelika. Ask counsel before relying on either.

**Incentives.** Opelika's council unanimously approved a 10-year abatement on state and local non-educational property taxes for a $79 million Faith Technologies manufacturing project (200+ jobs) at the former Joann Fabrics distribution center. Council members and the company said rumors that it was a data center were inaccurate ([WTVM, Feb. 25, 2026](https://www.wtvm.com/2026/02/25/data-center-expansion-manufacturing-project-expected-bring-hundreds-jobs-lee-county/)). It shows the abatement mechanism and the rumor risk.

**Outlook.** The record suggests the next fight, if one comes, would be a rezoning or incentive vote rather than a statewide rule.

## 3. Public sentiment

**What the data shows.** The tracker has 47 saved posts for Opelika from May 1 to Nov. 4: 36 on Facebook and 11 from meeting minutes. By stance, 29 are against, 15 supportive, 2 mixed and 1 neutral. These are people who post online, not residents. Engagement measures reach, not agreement. Most posts are comments under one October Facebook thread, so they are not a sample of the town.

**The main concerns, explained.**
- **Water (7 posts: 3 against, 2 supportive, 1 mixed).** Speakers worry about use; the 2 million gallon figure comes from another city's project. I could not check Opelika's supply, so it is unverified for here.
- **Rates (7: 3 against, 2 supportive, 1 mixed).** One commenter expects power bills to rise. Alabama's new PSC review is meant to test whether big contracts cover their costs, but it is untested and its reach to Opelika's utility is unconfirmed.
- **Jobs (7: 2 against, 3 supportive, 1 mixed).** Supporters ask, in one comment, "Why would you want to stop jobs". The only checkable local figure is 200+ jobs at the Faith Technologies manufacturing project, which is not a data center.
- **Power (5: 2 against, 2 supportive, 1 mixed).** Supporters say demand drives construction; opponents point to bills. Checkable only with utility data I lack.

Much of the October thread rests on rumor: the claim of "multiple data centers" has no named project I could find.

## 4. Other good-to-knows

- **Auburn:** Core Scientific has an existing data center in Auburn (AUB-I-X, Samford Avenue) with a $55 million building permit for an expansion that adds cooling systems and takes staffing from 11 to at least 20 ([WTVM](https://www.wtvm.com/2026/02/25/data-center-expansion-manufacturing-project-expected-bring-hundreds-jobs-lee-county/)).
- **Template for abatements:** the 10-year abatement for Faith Technologies shows how Opelika has used tax incentives for industrial reuse.
- **Statewide:** towns from Birmingham to Fort Payne have paused, and Bessemer voters ousted council members who approved the 1,200 MW Project Marvel (tracker state summary).
- **Dates to watch:** the PSC's 90-day review windows under the new law, council agendas for any zoning or moratorium item, and the end of the 2026-2027 city planning moratorium period. I could not confirm exact dates.
