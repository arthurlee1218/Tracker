# Siloam Springs, Benton County, AR: Data Center Siting Brief

As of 2026-11-06 · Built from Data Center Watch entries and sentiment data, checked against public reporting · Not legal advice; confirm with local counsel

**Bottom line.** Siloam Springs' Board of Directors approved the first reading of a six-month data center moratorium on Oct. 6, 2026, by 4-1.[^1] The tracker records second reading on Oct. 20 and final adoption on Nov. 3, both 5-2, but I could not open the city minutes or the Facebook post behind those two votes, and I found no news report of the final vote.[^2][^3] The biggest legal question is whether Arkansas Act 851 of 2023, written mostly about crypto mining, stops a city from pausing or banning data centers; the city's own law firm says the board has authority, while the sponsoring director reportedly said state law limits what a city can prohibit.[^1][^3] Two small developers, Kratos AI and Mavria, had been in contact with the city about 20 to 50 MW and 10 MW sites, and both said they were waiting on the board's decision.[^1] Next: the city has about six months to write rules, and a statewide fight over Act 851 may come in the 2027 session.[^4]

## 1. Legal analysis

**Local status.** On Tuesday, Oct. 6, 2026 the board approved first reading 4-1. Director Lesa Rissler introduced the ordinance. Director Brad Burns voted no; Directors David Allen and Mindy Hunt were absent. Final reading was set for Nov. 3.[^1] Per the tracker, second reading of Ordinance 26-25 passed 5-2 on Oct. 20 after ten residents spoke, all against data centers; two directors voted no and some worried about limiting economic opportunity.[^2] Per the tracker, the board adopted the moratorium 5-2 on Nov. 3.[^3] I could not open either source, and I could not find the names of the two no-voters or of the residents who spoke. The Oct. 6 minutes are a scanned image with no text, and the Oct. 20 and Nov. 3 minutes were not posted when the researchers checked. The ordinance text was not available to me.

**Zoning authority.** The city adopted a new Unified Development Code (City Code Chapter 35) in November 2025, effective Dec. 12, 2025.[^5] City pages indicate a special use permit route that needs Planning Commission and Board approval for some uses in industrial districts. I could not confirm whether data centers are a listed use, so treat that as unverified.

**Citizen initiatives.** Arkansas municipal referendum petitions are reported to be due 30 to 90 days after an ordinance passes, and only legislative acts can be referred.[^6] I did not confirm municipal initiative signature thresholds, and I found no petition in Siloam Springs.

**Water, incentives and disclosure.** Act 548 of 2025 widened the state sales and use tax exemption for data centers, cutting the qualifying investment from $500 million to $100 million and adding a large-data-center tier.[^7] Figures differ between summaries. I found no data-center-specific water or disclosure rule; general water-use registration applies at 50,000 gallons a day.[^8]

**Outlook.** Sen. Fred Love filed a non-binding interim resolution asking for a statewide moratorium; I did not confirm its status beyond committee referral, and Gov. Sanders's office has not backed one.[^9]

**Does Act 851 limit a city's data center moratorium?** Act 851 of 2023 (HB1799) is titled "To create the Arkansas Data Centers Act of 2023", and also covers digital asset mining.[^10] I could not read the body text. *The case that it limits cities:* Little Rock City Attorney Tom Carpenter said the title and the few data-center references could be enough for a judge to rule against a locality that bans data centers, and that a court fight could take years.[^11] Pulaski County acting county attorney Hamilton Kemp advised the Quorum Court it lacks authority to pass a moratorium, citing Act 851 and Attorney General Tim Griffin's November 2023 opinion.[^12] That opinion concluded that cities and counties cannot block a new crypto mining operation by ordinance; it addressed crypto, not data centers, and Griffin's office will not say whether Act 851 covers hyperscale data centers without a formal request.[^11] *The case that it does not:* Arkansas Municipal League general counsel John Wilkerson said "I don't see it", called the language murky, and said a city would need a good reason to ban an industry.[^11] Act 851 co-sponsor Sen. Josh Bryant (R-Rogers) said the intent was to prevent discrimination against both industries, that whether to ban data centers is for the state, and that locals can regulate issues around them. Lead sponsor Rep. Rick McClure said "I cannot say that" when asked if it applies to new data centers.[^11] Locally, Heather Owens of Harrington Miller Law Firm, the city's legal services provider, told the board it has authority to pass the ordinance; the Democrat-Gazette summarizes Act 851 as barring outright bans while allowing zoning and operating standards.[^1] *What is unsettled:* whether a time-limited pause counts as a ban. Pulaski JP Julie Blackwood called her county's measure "not a ban, it's a moratorium", while Kemp disagreed.[^12] *What would settle it:* a formal AG opinion, a court ruling, or a clarifying bill. Pulaski County officials expect a lawsuit if AVAIO's project is covered by its ordinance, which County Judge Barry Hyde vetoed.[^13]

## 2. What matters most here

**Wastewater capacity.** The city's plant treats about 2.6 million gallons a day on average, with storm peaks above 4.4 million, under an ADEQ discharge permit.[^14] Utility Commission member Bernadette Keck raised the wastewater upgrades a data center would need.[^1] State environmental official Bryan Leamons said in 2024 that Siloam Springs has a permit application or issued permit that EPA lawsuit negotiations could affect.[^15] I could not find the current permit's nutrient limits.

**A municipal power system with a 70 MW peak.** The city's Electric Department had 9,835 customers as of September 2024, four substations and a July 2023 peak of 70 MW; it owns no generation.[^16] It buys from the Grand River Dam Authority under a contract that runs 1989 to 2042, per electric director Phil Stokes.[^17] A 20 to 50 MW request would be large against a 70 MW peak, though I found no capacity statement from the city or GRDA, and no large-load tariff.

**Rates already rising.** On Dec. 16, 2025 the board approved 6-0 a schedule with 4% yearly increases for electric, water and wastewater from 2027 to 2030, after 4% water and wastewater in 2026; interim city administrator Christina Petriches estimated about $8 a month more for the average resident in 2026.[^18] Rates is the second-most raised concern in saved posts.

**Water source.** The city draws from the Illinois River in Oklahoma, with three supplemental reservoirs, and serves about 18,000 people.[^19] The city page does not state plant capacity, and I found no daily use figure. A consultant page cites 9 MGD, which I could not open.

**A lawmaker on both sides of the table.** Randy Torres is president and CEO of the Siloam Springs Chamber of Commerce and a state representative; he told the board he sits on the Joint Energy Committee.[^1] I did not find his position on the moratorium.

## 4. Public sentiment

**What the data shows.** The tracker holds 65 saved posts for Siloam Springs from May 1 to Nov. 4, 2026: 64 on Facebook and one council-minutes item. By stance, 53 are against, 6 supportive, 4 mixed and 2 neutral. These are people who post online, not residents, and engagement shows reach, not agreement. Statewide, 274 Arkansas posts are 247 against and 7 supportive.[^20]

**The main concerns, explained.**
- *Water* (16 posts, 15 against, 1 mixed). People worry about use and contamination; one comment sarcastically says town water is "about to get much tastier".[^21] The facts do not yet settle this: Mavria did not know its water need.[^1]
- *Rates* (9 posts, 8 against, 1 mixed). A top comment says "I'd rather have a Costco than a data center."[^21] Rates are already scheduled to rise 4% a year.[^18]
- *Noise* (8 posts, all against) and *tech* (7, all against). I found no local sound data.
- *Pause* (6 posts, 5 against): one comment says "Six-month moratorium isn't long enough."[^21]

**Voices on each side.** The Facebook pages needed a login, so I could not read commenter names or profiles: name not visible without logging in. All top posts are from individual accounts, per the tracker. Against: the Costco comment, Oct. 10, 2026, engagement 45/2 (top 4% on its platform), under an Arkansas Democrat-Gazette / ArkansasOnline.com Facebook post, which is an outlet's page; and a comment on water contamination, Oct. 19, 30/5, in the Facebook group "What's Up, Siloam?", whose operator the page does not state.[^21][^22] Supportive: "Everyone who reacted on this used a data center." Oct. 10, 6/9, and a comment saying to build it and tax it heavily, Oct. 10, 9/6.[^21] Reply readings were not available. Mixed comments ask for on-site power generation or payments to residents.[^21]

**What offline signals add.** Per the tracker, ten residents spoke against on Oct. 20; I could not get their names.[^2] An August 2026 Talk Business & Politics/Hendrix poll reportedly found over half of Arkansans strongly opposed to a data center in their community, but I could only see a search snippet, and the margin of error was reported inconsistently.[^23] The strongest supportive argument in the record is economic: some directors worried about limiting opportunity, and a supportive commenter points to jobs and says zoning and elected officials already handle siting.[^2][^21]

## 5. Other good-to-knows

- Fayetteville (Washington County) passed data center rules 8-0 on June 16, 2026, then added noise, water and energy limits on Aug. 18.[^24]
- Madison County passed a three-year moratorium in mid-August 2026; Independence County placed a five-year pause to Aug. 31, 2031.[^25][^26]
- Little Rock Director Kathy Webb proposed a six-month moratorium after an 18-month version tied 4-4 in August 2026.[^13]
- I found no moratoria in Bentonville, Rogers, Springdale or Benton County itself.
- Dates to watch: the city's rule-writing during the pause, the 2027 legislative session, and any formal AG opinion on Act 851.[^4]
