# Siloam Springs, Benton County, AR: Data Center Siting Brief

As of 2026-11-06 · Built from Data Center Watch entries and sentiment data, checked against public reporting · Not legal advice; confirm with local counsel

**Bottom line.** Siloam Springs' Board of Directors adopted a six-month data center moratorium on Nov. 3, 2026, on a 5-2 final vote, according to a board director's Facebook post logged by the tracker.[^1] The city had been in talks with two companies, Kratos AI and Mavria, about facilities of 10 to 50 megawatts, and both were waiting on the ordinance.[^2] The biggest legal question is Arkansas Act 851 of 2023. It was written mostly for crypto mining, and nobody has settled whether it stops a city from banning data centers.[^3] The city's own attorney told the board it may regulate location and operations but not ban outright.[^2] The city runs its own electric utility, so the city is both regulator and power seller.[^4] I could not open the Nov. 3 post or the Oct. 20 minutes, so the final vote tally rests on the tracker's record.

## 1. Legal analysis

**Local status.** Oct. 6, 2026: the board approved first reading of the six-month moratorium. The Democrat-Gazette reports 4-1, with Brad Burns voting no and David Allen and Mindy Hunt absent. Director Lesa Rissler introduced it.[^2] Oct. 20: second reading of Ordinance 26-25 passed 5-2 on a roll call after ten residents spoke, all against data centers. Two directors voted no, and the tracker's summary says some worried about limiting economic opportunity.[^5] The minutes name the speakers, but the file was too large for me to open, so I do not list them. Nov. 3: final adoption, 5-2, per a post from a board director on the Siloam Springs Citizen's Page. That director said Arkansas law limits what a city can prohibit and urged residents to contact state legislators.[^1] I could not name the two "no" votes on Oct. 20 or Nov. 3 (Burns voted no on Oct. 6). I tried the Facebook post (login wall) and the agenda packet (file over size limit). The Herald-Leader's story on the final vote did not appear in my searches.

**Zoning authority.** The city board adopts the moratorium for land inside city limits. Heather Owens of Harrington Miller Law Firm told the board it has authority to pass the ordinance. She said Act 851 bars an outright ban but lets cities regulate location and operations through zoning, planning approvals and operating standards.[^2] Rissler said the pause would give the city time to write policies, calling it "only six months."[^2]

**Citizen initiatives.** I could not confirm the current rules for a local referendum petition against Ordinance 26-25, and I found no report of one being filed. Arkansas Amendment 7 covers local referendums, but I did not open its text or the Secretary of State's handbook, so I make no claim about deadlines or signature counts.

**Water, incentives and disclosure.** Bernadette Keck, a member of the Siloam Springs Utility Commission, raised concern about upgrades a data center would need at the wastewater treatment facility.[^2] Mavria says it would use a closed-loop water system but has not said how much water it needs.[^2] I did not find state incentive or disclosure rules specific to this place.

**Outlook.** The moratorium runs six months from adoption, which points to roughly early May 2027 if it took effect on Nov. 3 (my arithmetic; the effective date was not in the sources I opened). Randy Torres, state representative and Chamber president, said he sits on the state Joint Energy Committee, which is meeting soon, and that he would tell the board the date.[^2]

**Does Act 851 stop a city from banning data centers?** The act created the "Arkansas Data Centers Act of 2023" and clarified regulation of digital asset mining.[^3] Attorney General Tim Griffin's November 2023 opinion said cities and counties have only powers given by constitution or statute, and that a city or county cannot ban new digital asset mining.[^6]

- *It limits cities:* Little Rock City Attorney Tom Carpenter said the title and a handful of references to data centers could be enough for a judge to rule against a locality, and that a court fight could take years.[^6] Unnamed county and city lawyers have cited the AG opinion as a reason restrictions might not survive.[^6] The tracker notes Little Rock's mayor reads it as meaning cities cannot ban data centers.[^7]
- *It does not:* John Wilkerson, general counsel and legislative director of the Arkansas Municipal League, said he does not think the law applies to data centers as it does to crypto mining, calling the language murky.[^6] Sen. Josh Bryant (R-Rogers), a co-sponsor, said bans should be left to the state while localities can regulate.[^6] Lead sponsor Rep. Rick McClure (R-Malvern) said he was unsure whether it covers new data centers.[^6]
- *Unsettled:* The AG's office said it would not say whether Act 851 covers hyperscale data centers without a formal opinion request.[^6] A court ruling, an AG opinion or a new bill would settle it. I found no case involving Siloam Springs.

## 2. What matters most here

**The city sells the power.** Siloam Springs Electric Department is publicly owned, operated and governed by the community since 1903, with 9,351 customers as of December 2024, 187.2 miles of distribution line, 10 miles of 69 kV transmission and four substations. Director Phil Stokes leads it.[^4] The city's page does not name its wholesale supplier, and I could not find it. Large-load tariffs for the city system: I could not confirm any exist.

**City staff are scouting sites.** Stokes suggested two locations to the companies: North Country Club Road near La-Z-Boy and Webb Wheel Products, and South Lincoln Street near Jetstream by Pipelife.[^2] The documents came from a FOIA request, so the pitch was already in motion before the first vote.[^2]

**Wastewater is the named water concern.** Utility Commission member Keck focused on wastewater plant upgrades.[^2] Neither company has published a water figure; Mavria says closed loop but gave no volume.[^2]

**A legislator sits on the state energy committee.** Torres wears two hats, Chamber CEO and state representative, and said the Joint Energy Committee was meeting soon.[^2] Rissler's request, and the board director's post urging residents to contact legislators, point to Little Rock as the place where limits on city power would change.[^1][^2]

## 4. Public sentiment

**What the data shows.** From 2026-05-01 to 2026-11-04 the tracker has 65 posts for the city: 64 Facebook, one meeting-minutes entry. Stances: 53 against, 6 supportive, 4 mixed, 2 neutral.[^9] These are people who post online, not residents, and engagement reflects reach.

**The main concerns, explained.** Water is the top topic: 16 posts, 15 against, 1 mixed.[^9] Rates: 9 posts, 8 against, 1 mixed. One top comment says "I'd rather have a Costco than a data center."[^10] Noise: 8, all against. Technology: 7, all against. Jobs: 5, four against and one supportive; a supportive commenter says it "will bring jobs to the community."[^11] Pause comments are 6, five against; one says a "six-month moratorium isn't long enough."[^12] The pro side is small and often about tax revenue.

**Voices on each side.** All accounts are individual Facebook commenters; the names are on the page and not stored, so I give no names. Against: a commenter on an Arkansas Democrat-Gazette post (Oct. 10), 45 reactions and 2 replies, preferring a Costco to a data center;[^10] another on the same post (Oct. 10), 34 reactions, wanting a longer pause.[^12] A commenter in a Facebook group (Oct. 19), 30 reactions and 5 replies, raised water use and contamination.[^13] In favor: a commenter on the Democrat-Gazette post (Oct. 10), 6 reactions and 9 replies, saying everyone who reacted used a data center;[^14] another (Oct. 10), 9 reactions and 6 replies, suggesting building and taxing it.[^15] I could not read reply stances or profile pages, since the pages need a login.

**What offline signals add.** At the Oct. 20 meeting ten residents spoke, all against.[^5] I could not open the minutes to name them. The two no votes show a minority worried about economic opportunity.[^5] The strongest case for the other side: the 20 and 50 MW proposals are far from hyperscale, and Chamber CEO Torres sits on a state energy committee.[^2] No polls found.

## 5. Other good-to-knows

- Arkansas has no statewide data center rules, and counties elsewhere have passed long pauses, while Little Rock twice rejected a moratorium on 4-4 ties.[^7]
- In September 2026 lawmakers questioned Entergy over charging ratepayers for Google-linked solar for West Memphis.[^16]
- SWEPCO's Flint Creek coal plant near Gentry is in the area; I did not confirm its relevance to any project.
- Other Benton Counties exist (Tennessee, Missouri); do not mix up their moratorium news with Benton County, Arkansas. I found no Arkansas county pause for this county.
