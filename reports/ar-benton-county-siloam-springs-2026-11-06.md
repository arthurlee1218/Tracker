# Siloam Springs, Benton County, AR: Data Center Siting Brief

As of 2026-11-06 · Built from Data Center Watch entries and sentiment data, checked against public reporting · Not legal advice; confirm with local counsel

**Bottom line.** Siloam Springs' board adopted a six-month data center moratorium on Nov. 3, 2026, by a 5-2 final vote, after two earlier readings. The city's stated purpose is to write development rules, and the sponsoring director called it "not a permanent moratorium." Two small developers had approached the city before the pause, and both said they were waiting on its outcome. The biggest legal lever is state law: Arkansas Act 851 of 2023 is read by local officials as barring outright bans, though whether it covers data centers is unsettled. Zoning and operating standards remain open to the city. Legislators are expected to take up local authority over data centers next spring. The moratorium expires around early May 2027 unless the city extends it or adopts rules sooner.

## 1. Legal analysis

**Local status.** The board passed the first reading 4-1 on Oct. 6 and the second reading 5-2 on Oct. 20 (Ordinance 26-25). It adopted the ordinance on Nov. 3 by 5-2, per the [Data Center Watch record](https://www.siloamsprings.gov/AgendaCenter/ViewFile/Agenda/_11032026-948) (the city's own agenda packet; I could not open it because it was too large). Director Lesa Rissler introduced it to give the city time to write policy, per the [Democrat-Gazette](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2026/oct/09/siloam-springs-city-board-approves-first-reading/). A local attorney told the board it has authority to pass the ordinance.

**State preemption.** The [Democrat-Gazette](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2026/jun/07/arkansas-officials-ask-whether-2023-law-on-crypto/) reports that Act 851, titled the Arkansas Data Centers Act of 2023, mainly addresses crypto mining. It bars rezoning aimed at discriminating against digital-asset mining and lets those businesses operate in industrial zones. City and county lawyers have cited a November 2023 Attorney General opinion to argue local bans would not hold up. The Little Rock city attorney said a judge could read the act to cover data centers. Arkansas Municipal League counsel said he does not see that reading. The lead sponsor said he was unsure it covers new data centers. A court challenge might be needed to settle it, and it could take years. The Herald-Leader coverage states the act bars outright bans but allows local zoning and operating standards. I could not confirm any court ruling on the point.

**Zoning authority.** The city board controls zoning inside city limits. Siloam Springs has not yet adopted data-center-specific rules that I could confirm.

**Citizen initiatives.** I could not confirm local initiative or referendum rules or any petition activity on this topic.

**Water law, incentives, disclosure.** I could not confirm any Arkansas statute on data center water use, large-load tariffs or disclosure that applies here.

**Outlook.** The [Arkansas Advocate](https://arkansasadvocate.com/2026/11/02/data-center-backlash-playing-out-in-arkansas-local-races-not-statewide-or-federal-campaigns/) was cited in search results as saying the laws municipalities say tie their hands will be among the biggest topics for legislators next spring (I could not open the article itself). The Siloam Springs chamber CEO, who is also a state representative, told the board the state Joint Energy Committee would meet soon.

## 3. Public sentiment

**What the data shows.** The tracker holds 65 posts for this place from 2026-05-01 to 2026-11-04: 64 from Facebook and one from meeting minutes. By stance, 53 are against, 6 supportive, 4 mixed and 2 neutral. These are people who post online, not residents. Engagement measures reach, not agreement. Most posts are comments under a few threads (a newspaper page and a community group), so they are not a sample of the town.

**The main concerns, explained.**
- **Water (16 posts: 15 against, 1 mixed).** Commenters raise water use and contamination, such as one asking "what about the water usage and contamination?" and a sarcastic line about the water getting "tastier." This is partly checkable. A developer did not know its water need, and a utility member's concern was about wastewater. I could not confirm the source or capacity.
- **Rates (9 posts: 8 against, 1 mixed).** Posters expect costs to rise, for example "If you think stuff is expensive now, just wait." Whether a data center would raise local rates depends on the city utility's contracts, which I could not confirm.
- **Noise (8 posts, all against) and tech (7, all against).** These are general worries, not tied to a documented project plan.
- **Land and jobs (7 and 5 posts).** Supporters say it would bring jobs and that zoning and elected officials already provide protection. Opponents prefer other businesses, such as one wanting a Costco instead.
